Cygnet House · Internal design reference

Qualifier form — four luxury treatments

All four are the same three-step form on the same token names as index.html, so whichever you pick pastes straight across. Selection auto-advances in every variant — tap an answer and the step moves itself. Try each one; the differences are in how the choice feels, not in what it asks.

On auto-advance: it fires on tap and click only. Arrow keys move between radios in a group and fire a change event at every stop, so advancing on keyboard selection would throw a keyboard user past the question the instant they started reading it. Keyboard users get the Continue button — which is why it stays in all four. There is a 260 ms beat before the step changes so the tick is visibly acknowledged rather than swallowed by the transition.

F1 · Ink Atelier Shipped

The card itself goes ink, so the form stops being a widget on the hero and becomes the centre of gravity of the page. Reads private-clinic, not high-street. Costs you the white-card familiarity that makes forms feel safe to fill in — worth A/B testing against F2 once there is traffic to test with.

F2 · Gilded Hairline Runner-up

Luxury by restraint: 1px hairlines instead of 2px boxes, a gradient rule replacing the 4px slab, a small-caps step counter, and answers as a divided list with a chevron that slides in. Keeps the white card working for you while removing everything that read as "form widget". Lowest-risk of the four — the natural control to test F1 against.

F3 · Concierge Tiles Warmest

Lettered chips, rows that lift on hover, a pill CTA and a crest at the top. Feels like a concierge questionnaire rather than a lead form — the friendliest of the four for nervous patients, which is the audience §8 of the page is written for. Slightly more "app" than "atelier".

F4 · Editorial Quietest

No boxes at all: underline-only fields, a large display headline, wide letter-spaced labels and a lot of air. The most expensive-looking of the four and the most demanding — thin borders and low-contrast labels need real discipline, and underline fields test slightly worse for completion on mobile than bordered ones.
